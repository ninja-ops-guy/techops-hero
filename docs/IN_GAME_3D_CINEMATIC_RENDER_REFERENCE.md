## Render language

- **Characters:** Preserve recognizable approved silhouettes before adding detail. Iona has short black hair with bangs, ear piercings and her dark work jacket. K has a beanie, over-ear headphones, clear glasses and K insignia. Do not import incidental face drift, costume changes, or generated text as character canon. Kali is a long-haired black cat; his paws must remain black in the game model.
- **Materials:** Use tangible cloth, worn leather, metal, glass, skin and wet stone responses. In vehicles, instrument panels should cast motivated light onto hands and faces while glass reflects the surrounding world. Layer roughness and normal detail at the scale seen in medium shots; prioritize face, hands, hair, clothing folds and the contact points the camera actually reveals. Keep dark clothing separated from dark backgrounds with controlled rim light.
- **Lighting:** Treat orange fire, sunset and lamps as motivated warm sources; contrast them with violet dusk or cool industrial interior light. Bounce, haze and reflections should explain the environment rather than flatten it. Preserve readable eyes and facial planes in the close-ups. Explosion brightness and reflection should evolve together over time.
- **Camera:** Stage the location in a wide shot, establish spatial direction in a medium shot, then use close-ups when a reaction changes the meaning. For orbital sequences, transition from cockpit detail to a wide exterior with consistent vehicle direction and scale. Follow Iona's action on a legible trajectory; hold long enough on K and Iona's reunion for the gesture to read. Compose in 16:9 gameplay presentation with a 9:16 safe crop for the portrait references; avoid copying portrait framing so tightly that UI or subtitles have no room.
- **Motion:** Show anticipation, planted foot or hand contact, acceleration, follow-through and recovery. Hand-to-control motion should line up with the control's pivot or travel, and the vehicle response should follow its input. Crouch and jump must feel connected to the same ground plane. Avoid foot sliding, hand penetration, floating embraces and abrupt pose snaps. Give hair, jacket and straps secondary motion without overwhelming the pose. Eye-line, blink and breathing should carry quiet moments; lip sync only to approved dialogue and audio.
- **Environments and effects:** Build a camera-consistent 3D location with real occlusion and usable depth. Cockpit windows, station debris and passing ships need coherent parallax and lighting as the camera moves. Wet surfaces, smoke, distant activity and embers can add depth. Use restrained depth of field and bloom so silhouettes, contact points and captions remain clear. Keep particles and explosive effects tied to known world positions and light sources.

## Scene and engine integration

1. Start with the approved comic beat and the matching mission trigger. Storyboard its entry, key acting pose, camera change and exit to play. Reuse a source panel or motion idea when it serves that beat, then adapt it to game camera and controls. Use a match frame between the final cutscene frame and the first playable frame, including horizon, actor placement and lighting direction.
2. Reuse the existing campaign and cinematic owners. The render/animation layer observes story state and plays a shot; it does not decide damage, collision, objective completion, saves, film recovery or input ownership. Skip, reduced-motion behavior, subtitles, audio priority, pause and a clean return to control must remain available.
3. The current post-K Good Dogs 3D level has a stylized GLB prototype with quality tiers and a presentation-only renderer. Treat this brief as the quality target for the next character, rig, lighting and cinematic pass, not as a claim that the prototype already matches the supplied close-ups. See [post-K 3D](GOOD_DOGS_POST_K_3D.md) and [fidelity limits](GOOD_DOGS_3D_FIDELITY.md).
4. Preserve the approved Night Walker sprite and environment grammar for normal 2D play. In-engine 3D cinematics can use this richer material and camera treatment, provided the characters, palette motifs and handoff back to gameplay are unmistakably the same world. Comic-panel framing in the laundromat reference is an optional authored transition, not a requirement to render gameplay as a comic page.
